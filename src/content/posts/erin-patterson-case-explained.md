Three people died after a family lunch in Leongatha, Victoria, in July 2023. They were Don Patterson, Gail Patterson and Heather Wilkinson. Ian Wilkinson survived. In July 2025, a jury found Erin Patterson guilty of three murders and one attempted murder. On Oct. 30, 2026, Victoria's Court of Appeal dismissed her appeal. The same court raised her sentence to life in prison without parole.

## Who they were

The lunch was on July 29, 2023, at Erin Patterson's home in Leongatha. That is a small town about 135 kilometers southeast of Melbourne ([Al Jazeera, July 7, 2025](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/7/7/australian-woman-guilty-of-mushroom-murders-all-to-know)).

- **Don Patterson**, 70, was her father-in-law.
- **Gail Patterson**, 70, was her mother-in-law.
- **Heather Wilkinson**, 66, was Gail's sister.
- **Ian Wilkinson**, 71, was Heather's husband and a church pastor. He survived after weeks in a coma in the hospital. He later testified at the trial.

Don and Gail were the parents of her estranged husband, Simon Patterson. He was invited but did not go ([Al Jazeera, July 7, 2025](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/7/7/australian-woman-guilty-of-mushroom-murders-all-to-know)).

They were parents, grandparents, a wife and a husband. This case is about them first.

One route is still open. She can ask Australia's highest court to hear her case. As of Oct. 31, 2026, no such request has been reported.

## The trial and the first sentence

Erin Patterson pleaded not guilty to all charges. Her lawyers told the jury the deaths were a terrible accident. They said she panicked and lied to police about where the mushrooms came from. They said she got sick from the meal too ([Al Jazeera, July 7, 2025](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/7/7/australian-woman-guilty-of-mushroom-murders-all-to-know)).

The jury did not accept that. On July 7, 2025, the Supreme Court of Victoria jury found her guilty on all four counts.

On September 8, 2025, Justice Christopher Beale gave her three life sentences. Under that first sentence, she had to serve at least 33 years before she could ask for parole ([ABC News Australia, Aug. 21, 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-08-21/key-takeaways-erin-patterson-appeal-murder-conviction/107059510)). The Court of Appeal replaced that sentence on Oct. 30, 2026.

## The appeals: what was argued

Two appeals were heard together in the Court of Appeal in Melbourne on August 19–20, 2026. Three judges heard them: Justices Lesley Taylor, Stephen McLeish and Peter Kidd ([ABC News Australia, Aug. 20, 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-08-20/erin-patterson-appeal-mushroom-murder-court/107059860)).

**Her appeal (August 19).** Her lawyers raised seven grounds. They said mistakes at trial led to a miscarriage of justice. Key points reported by ABC News Australia ([Aug. 21, 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-08-21/key-takeaways-erin-patterson-appeal-murder-conviction/107059510)):

- **The jury hotel.** During deliberations, jurors stayed at the same hotel as a police informant and some prosecution lawyers. The defense called this a "catastrophic" error. Prosecutors called the argument "far-fetched."
- **Photos on an SD card.** The trial judge first kept some photos out of evidence. A few days later, he let them in. The defense said that was wrong.

Prosecutors said the case against her was "overwhelming" ([ABC News Australia, Aug. 20, 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-08-20/erin-patterson-appeal-mushroom-murder-court/107059860)).

**The prosecution's appeal (August 20).** The Director of Public Prosecutions said her sentence was too light. Prosecutors asked for life with no chance of parole. They argued the crimes were in the "worst category" ([ABC News Australia, Aug. 20, 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-08-20/erin-patterson-appeal-mushroom-murder-court/107059860)).

## What the court decided on Oct. 30, 2026

The three judges gave their decision in Melbourne on Friday, Oct. 30, 2026, at 10 a.m. local time ([Supreme Court of Victoria](https://www.supremecourt.vic.gov.au/news/erin-patterson-appeals-livestreamed)). Justice McLeish read the reasons in court ([ABC News Australia, Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-10-30/erin-patterson-appeal-decision-live-updates/107322086)).

- **Her appeal: dismissed.** The court let her argue two of her seven grounds: the jury's accommodation and the use of her mobile phone data as evidence. It rejected both. It refused to hear the other five. Her convictions stand.
- **The prosecution's appeal: allowed.** The court set aside the 33-year non-parole period. It re-sentenced her to life in prison without parole. Justice McLeish said parole was not appropriate in this case ([ABC News Australia, Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-10-30/erin-patterson-appeal-decision-live-updates/107322086)).

This summary comes from the court's own notice and ABC's report from the courtroom. We have not yet read the full written judgment.

## What happens next

Her remaining route is the High Court of Australia. She would first need the court's permission to appeal, called special leave. Legal experts quoted by ABC say the High Court takes very few cases ([ABC News Australia, Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-10-30/erin-patterson-mushroom-murder-appeal-what-next/107315286)).

A second appeal in Victoria is possible only with fresh and compelling evidence. An expert quoted by ABC called that "most unlikely" in her case ([ABC News Australia, Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-10-30/erin-patterson-mushroom-murder-appeal-what-next/107315286)).

As of Oct. 31, 2026, no request to the High Court has been reported. We will not guess what happens next. See the [Court Calendar entry](/court-calendar/#patterson-appeal) for the latest status we have checked.
