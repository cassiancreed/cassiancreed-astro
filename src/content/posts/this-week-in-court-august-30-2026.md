Five major criminal proceedings move forward this week across the United States. Each case involves gravity and significant public interest, but each sits at a different stage of the judicial process.

This week, the legal landscape is defined by five distinct tasks: a jury's final deliberations, closing arguments, the continued presentation of federal evidence, an arraignment after a judge held a defendant to answer, and a judicial determination of probable cause.

This briefing covers court activity scheduled from Sunday, August 30, through Saturday, September 5, 2026. The reporting cutoff is Saturday afternoon, August 29. Court schedules are fluid—particularly when juries are deliberating—so dates and times may change. Every defendant discussed below faces unresolved charges and is presumed innocent. Allegations and prosecution claims are not established facts unless admitted evidence or a final judgment establishes them.

**Update note — September 1, 2026:** This briefing is preserved as an August 29 snapshot. The Davis section now begins with a post-verdict update; the original preview remains below for historical context.

## Lindsay Clancy: Jury Deliberations Resume in Massachusetts

### Current posture

In Plymouth County Superior Court, *Commonwealth v. Lindsay Clancy* has reached jury deliberations. Clancy has pleaded not guilty to three counts of first-degree murder in the deaths of Cora, Dawson, and Callan Clancy. The defense contests criminal responsibility, and she remains presumed innocent.

Jurors completed a second full day of deliberations on Friday without reaching a verdict. They requested to review physical evidence. A jury's evidence request is part of the deliberative process; it does not reveal how any juror is voting or predict an outcome.

### Schedule

Deliberations are scheduled to resume **Monday, August 31, at 9:00 a.m. Eastern Time**.

### The question of criminal responsibility

The central legal dispute is criminal responsibility under Massachusetts law. A medical diagnosis does not by itself satisfy the legal test. Because lack of criminal responsibility has been raised, the Commonwealth must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that Clancy was criminally responsible. In general terms, that means proving that a mental disease or defect did not deprive her of the substantial capacity to appreciate the wrongfulness of her conduct or conform her conduct to the law.

### What to watch

- **Jury notes:** Any written communications from jurors to the judge.
- **Renewed instructions:** Whether the judge provides further legal guidance.
- **Verdict announcement:** Formal notice that the jury has reached a unanimous decision.

Deliberation length does not reliably predict a verdict, a deadlock, or a mistrial. For a fuller explanation, read [what happens when a jury deliberates](/post/what-actually-happens-when-a-jury-deliberates/) and the [Lindsay Clancy trial tracker](/post/lindsay-clancy-trial/).

## Duane “Keffe D” Davis: Closing Arguments in Nevada

### Verdict postscript — September 1, 2026

Tupac Shakur was killed in Las Vegas in 1996. On August 31, a Clark County jury found Duane “Keffe D” Davis guilty of **first-degree murder with the use of a deadly weapon**. Prosecutors did not contend that Davis personally fired the fatal shots; they argued that he orchestrated the killing and was criminally responsible under their principal-liability theory. The exact verdict form and jury instructions were not publicly available in the sources reviewed, so this postscript does not attribute a specific instruction to the jury. Sentencing is set for October 13, 2026. No hearing time has been publicly verified, and no sentence has been imposed.

Prosecutors said they would dismiss the separate gang-enhancement allegation because of witness-logistics problems; no written dismissal order or docket entry was reviewed for this postscript. Davis stated that he intends to appeal; this postscript does not characterize an appeal as pending.

### Original briefing posture — August 29 reporting cutoff

The trial of Duane “Keffe D” Davis in Clark County, Nevada, concerning Tupac Shakur's 1996 death is moving to closing arguments. The defense rested Thursday after calling three witnesses. Davis did not testify, as is his constitutional right.

Davis is charged with murder with the use of a deadly weapon and has pleaded not guilty. Prosecutors do not allege that he was the shooter. Their theory is that he is criminally responsible under Nevada law for allegedly aiding or abetting the offense. The jury must decide the charge from admitted evidence and the judge's instructions.

### Schedule

Closing arguments are scheduled for **Monday, August 31**. The start time has not been publicly verified.

### What to watch

- **Final jury instructions:** The legal framework jurors must apply.
- **The parties' framing:** How each side interprets the admitted evidence.
- **Start of deliberations:** The point at which the case is formally submitted to the jury.

Closing arguments are not evidence. Jurors also may not treat Davis's decision not to testify as evidence or as an inference of guilt. Read more about [how Nevada principal liability applied in the Davis case](/post/tupac-shakur-keffe-d-charged-not-shooter/).

## Durk Banks, Known as Lil Durk: Evidence and Credibility in Federal Court

### Current posture

The federal murder-for-hire conspiracy trial involving Durk Devontay Banks and two co-defendants continues in Los Angeles. All three defendants have pleaded not guilty. The charges are allegations, and each defendant is presumed innocent.

The case remains in the evidence phase, with the government presenting its case concerning the 2022 death of Saviay'a Robinson.

### Schedule

The exact next session date and time have not been publicly verified. Although the trial is underway, this briefing does not promise a Monday appearance without confirmation from the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California.

### The task of assessing credibility

The defendants are charged under the federal murder-for-hire statute, 18 U.S.C. § 1958. As prosecutors present witnesses, jurors must evaluate credibility, including the testimony of any cooperating witnesses. Cooperation does not make a witness automatically truthful or untruthful; jurors weigh the testimony against the rest of the admitted evidence and the judge's instructions.

Lyrics and a public persona do not independently prove criminal intent. Lawyers' questions are also not evidence; witnesses' sworn answers and admitted exhibits make up the trial record.

### What to watch

- **Trial calendar:** Confirmed sessions from the federal court's daily calendar.
- **Remaining testimony:** The witnesses called as the government continues its case.
- **Evidentiary rulings:** Decisions about which exhibits or statements jurors may consider.

## Celeste Rivas Hernandez Case: David Anthony Burke's Arraignment

### Current posture

After a five-day preliminary hearing, a Los Angeles County judge held David Anthony Burke, known as D4vd, to answer after finding sufficient probable cause for the case to proceed. Burke faces charges connected to the death of 14-year-old Celeste Rivas Hernandez. He has pleaded not guilty and remains presumed innocent.

The proceeding now moves to arraignment on the information. A holding order at a preliminary hearing is not a conviction and does not establish guilt beyond a reasonable doubt.

### Schedule

The arraignment on the information is scheduled for **Monday, August 31**. The time has not been publicly verified.

### The purpose of the arraignment

After a defendant is held to answer, California procedure requires prosecutors to file an information, the formal charging document used for the next phase of the case. At the arraignment, the information is presented, a plea is entered, and the court may address future dates and administrative scheduling.

### What to watch

- **The filed information:** The counts and enhancements formally presented at this stage.
- **Plea entry:** Burke's formal response to the information.
- **Trial setting:** Whether the court establishes the next major date.

## Charlie Kirk Case: Probable-Cause Argument for Tyler Robinson in Utah

### Current posture

In Utah's Fourth District Court, the preliminary-hearing process for Tyler James Robinson is approaching its conclusion. Robinson is charged with aggravated murder and related felonies following the fatal shooting of Charlie Kirk. The state has filed notice that it is seeking the death penalty. No plea has been entered. The charges are allegations, and Robinson remains presumed innocent.

### Schedule

Oral argument on bindover is scheduled for **Tuesday, September 1, at 10:00 a.m. Mountain Daylight Time**. The court has precluded electronic-media coverage of this session, so no livestream should be assumed.

### Defining probable cause

The judge must decide whether the preliminary-hearing evidence establishes probable cause to bind the charges over for trial under Utah law. Probable cause is a lower standard than proof beyond a reasonable doubt. A bindover order would allow the prosecution to continue; it would not be a conviction and would not predict how a trial jury might evaluate the evidence.

### What to watch

- **Timing of the ruling:** Whether the judge rules from the bench or takes the matter under advisement.
- **Charge decisions:** Whether any charged count is narrowed or dismissed.
- **Bindover order:** The formal decision on whether the case advances toward trial.
