**Status as of October 15, 2026, 3:47 p.m. Eastern:** Dale Smith Jr. is charged with second-degree murder in the 2011 disappearance of Michelle Parker. He pleaded not guilty on October 15 and is being held without bond. He is presumed innocent. Michelle has never been found. > **Quick answer:** Yes, Florida can charge murder without a body. The Florida Supreme Court has said a death caused by a crime can be proven with circumstantial evidence, "even without any evidence of the discovery of the victim's body." Florida also has no time limit for prosecuting a felony that caused a death. A jury still has to be convinced beyond a reasonable doubt.

## Who Michelle Parker Is

Michelle Parker was a 33-year-old mother from Orlando. On November 17, 2011, she was last seen after going to the home of her ex-fiancé, Dale Smith Jr.

That same day, a TV court show aired an episode with the couple. On *The People's Court*, taped months earlier, Smith had sued Parker over a $5,000 engagement ring. The judge ruled they were both responsible and ordered Parker to pay him $2,500, according to the Associated Press.

Her family reported her missing that day. For 15 years, they have asked for answers.

## The 15-Year Timeline

- **Nov. 17, 2011:** Michelle is last seen after going to Smith's home in Orlando's Lee Vista area. Her family reports her missing.
- **Nov. 18, 2011:** Police find her SUV abandoned in an Orlando parking lot.
- **Dec. 7, 2011:** Her cellphone is recovered from a lake south of Orlando.
- **Late 2011:** Within two weeks, police call Smith their primary suspect. He is not charged. He has long denied any wrongdoing.
- **Sept. 2022:** An anonymous donor raises the reward for information to up to $200,000.
- **Oct. 2025 to Oct. 2026:** Investigators re-examine old statements and evidence, using newer digital tools.
- **Oct. 14, 2026:** Orlando police arrest Smith at his workplace on one count of second-degree murder.
- **Oct. 15, 2026:** Smith pleads not guilty and is held without bond.

## What Police Say Changed

Orlando Police Chief Eric Smith called Dale Smith Jr. "the last known person to see or hear from Michelle."

Police have shared only a few details. They say they:

- re-examined witness statements and conflicting statements Smith gave over the years;
- used newer digital forensics on phones, computers and vehicles; and
- gathered new evidence in the past year.

The 46-page arrest affidavit is **sealed**, according to Smith's lawyer. That means the public cannot yet see the full evidence. Any claim about what it says should be treated with care until it is released.

## What the Defense Says

Smith's attorney, Rajan Joshi, told WFTV that he read the sealed affidavit but could not keep a copy. He said it mentions recovered text messages between Parker and Smith and a recent interview with one of Smith's friends. He called the case largely circumstantial and said he saw no "smoking gun." He said Smith maintains his innocence.

These are the defense's descriptions. The court has not ruled on the evidence.

## What Florida Law Allows

**No time limit.** Under Florida law, a prosecution "for a felony that resulted in a death may be commenced at any time" (Florida Statutes, section 775.15). So 15 years does not stop a murder charge.

**No body required.** In *Crain v. State* (2004), the Florida Supreme Court said the basic proof of a murder — that someone died because of another person's crime — "can be proven circumstantially, even without any evidence of the discovery of the victim's body." That court relied on earlier Florida rulings that say the same thing.

**What the charge means.** Second-degree murder in Florida is a death caused by an act "imminently dangerous to another and evincing a depraved mind" (section 782.04). It does not require proof of a plan to cause death. It is a first-degree felony, punishable by up to life in prison. State Attorney Monique Worrell said cases like this usually start at second-degree murder, and the charge could be raised if evidence supports it.

**The bar is still high.** A charge is not proof. To convict, prosecutors must convince a jury beyond a reasonable doubt. Cases with no body often rely on many small facts that must fit together.

## What Happens Next

1. **Bond hearing.** Smith is held without bond. His lawyer plans to ask for an "Arthur hearing," named for the 1980 Florida case *State v. Arthur*. At that hearing, prosecutors must show the "proof is evident or the presumption is great" to keep him jailed before trial. No date has been set.
2. **Evidence sharing.** The defense says it has not yet received the state's evidence. That exchange, called discovery, comes next.
3. **The affidavit.** The defense says prosecutors indicated it could be unsealed soon. When it is released, the new facts will appear on our Court Calendar with an as-of date.
