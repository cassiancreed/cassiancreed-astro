## Who Was Leslie Preer

Leslie Preer lived in Chevy Chase, Maryland. She was 49, according to charging documents cited by Montgomery Community Media; some news reports say 50. On May 2, 2001, she was found dead in her home in the 4800 block of Drummond Avenue. Her death was ruled a homicide. Her case stayed open for 23 years.

Eugene Gligor, the man who later pleaded guilty to second-degree murder in her death, was known to her family. He had dated her daughter years before.

## The Investigation

Police were called to the house late that morning, after Leslie did not show up for work. DNA from an unknown man was collected at the scene and kept.

No match was found for the male DNA, and the case went cold. For years, no database search found the man.

## How DNA Found Him

In September 2022, Montgomery County police sent evidence from the scene to Othram, a lab in The Woodlands, Texas. Othram built a DNA profile from it. Genetic genealogists then compared that profile with people who chose to share their DNA in online databases.

State's Attorney John McCarthy said that search led to a distant relative in Romania, and from there to Gligor. In June 2024, detectives collected DNA from a water bottle he threw away. It matched the DNA from the 2001 scene.

The genealogy gave police the lead. The direct DNA match was the proof. McCarthy called it the first time familial DNA was used to solve a cold case murder in Montgomery County.

## The Plea and the Sentence

Police got a warrant on June 15, 2024. U.S. Marshals arrested Gligor, then 44, in Washington, D.C., later that month, and he was charged with first-degree murder. Police give the arrest date as June 18; court records cited by Bethesda Magazine say June 21.

On May 7, 2025, before Judge David Lease, Gligor pleaded guilty to second-degree murder. In 2001, second-degree murder carried a maximum of 30 years in Maryland.

On Aug. 28, 2025, Judge Lease sentenced him to 30 years with all but 22 suspended. That means 22 years to serve, followed by five years of supervised probation. Prosecutors had asked for the full 30 years. State guidelines called for 10 to 18 years.

## Timeline

- **May 2, 2001:** Leslie Preer is found dead in her Chevy Chase home. DNA from an unknown man is collected.
- **2001 to 2022:** No database match. The case goes cold.
- **September 2022:** Police send the evidence to Othram for genetic genealogy work.
- **June 9, 2024:** Detectives collect Gligor's DNA and get a match.
- **June 15, 2024:** An arrest warrant is issued.
- **June 2024 (June 18 per police; June 21 per court records cited by Bethesda Magazine):** U.S. Marshals arrest Gligor in Washington, D.C. He is charged with first-degree murder.
- **May 7, 2025:** Gligor pleads guilty to second-degree murder.
- **Aug. 28, 2025:** He is sentenced to 30 years, all but 22 suspended, plus five years of supervised probation.

## Why This Case Matters

DNA kept from an old scene can still be used decades later. When the main police database finds no match, that is not always the end. Genealogy can point to a name, and a direct DNA test can then confirm it.
