## The Two Cases of Luigi Mangione

At 6:44 a.m. on December 4, 2024, the glass-and-steel anonymity of the healthcare elite was pierced by a 3D-printed ghost. Outside the New York Hilton Midtown, Brian Thompson, the CEO of UnitedHealthcare, was walking toward an investor conference when a masked figure emerged from between two parked cars. With a suppressed 9mm pistol, the gunman shot Thompson, then vanished into the morning mist of Central Park on an electric bicycle.

Luigi Mangione, then a 26-year-old University of Pennsylvania graduate, was later arrested. He was charged in two separate cases — one federal, one in New York state — over a killing that became a national Rorschach test. In August 2026 he pleaded guilty in the federal case. The state case is still pending.

Brian Thompson was 50. He was a husband and the father of two sons. He had worked at UnitedHealth for nearly 20 years and became CEO of UnitedHealthcare in 2021. His wife called him "an incredibly loving, generous, talented man" ([ABC News, Dec. 4, 2024](https://abcnews.com/news/story/unitedhealthcare-ceo-brian-thompson-116446881)). He is the reason this case matters.

## The Message on the Brass: "Delay, Deny, Depose"

Police said they recovered spent 9mm shell casings at the scene etched with three words: "delay," "deny," and "depose." Critics have long used the phrase "Delay, Deny, Defend" to describe the tactical playbook insurers use to avoid paying out legitimate claims. The words on the brass echoed that phrase, with "Depose" in place of "Defend."

## The Ghost in the Machine: What Prosecutors Allege

Mangione allegedly utilized a 3D-printed "ghost gun" — a Glock 19 specification known as the "FMDA 19.2" design — and a 3D-printed suppressor, allowing him to move through the city with an unserialized, untraceable firearm. Prosecutors say that, using a fraudulent New Jersey ID under the alias "Mark Rosario," he stayed at a Manhattan hostel for ten days, casing the Hilton and tracking Thompson's movements. He was eventually apprehended at a McDonald's in Altoona, Pennsylvania.

## The Manifesto: A Data-Driven Indictment

Police say that when he was arrested, Mangione was carrying a three-page, 262-word handwritten document addressed "To the Feds." It was less a rambling screed and more a cold, statistical indictment of American healthcare. He cited the immense wealth of UnitedHealth against the declining life expectancy of the average American, calling the industry a "greed-fueled health insurance cartel."

> "The US has the #1 most expensive healthcare system in the world, yet we rank roughly #42 in life expectancy... these parasites simply had it coming."

This document has been central to both prosecutions.

## The Two Cases: Federal and State

Status as of October 21, 2026:

**The federal case (Southern District of New York).** On August 14, 2026, Mangione pleaded guilty to Counts One and Two of the federal indictment ([court docket, *United States v. Mangione*, No. 1:25-cr-00176](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69908890/united-states-v-mangione/)). The U.S. Attorney's Office says those counts are interstate stalking resulting in death and cyberstalking through use of interstate facilities resulting in death. Each carries a maximum of life in prison. The judge decides the sentence ([U.S. Attorney's Office, Aug. 14, 2026](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/luigi-mangione-pleads-guilty-connection-his-murder-united-healthcare-ceo-brian)). Sentencing is set for **Friday, December 18, 2026, at 11:00 a.m. ET**, before Judge Margaret M. Garnett. No sentence has been imposed yet.

**The New York state case.** Mangione has pleaded not guilty to the state charges, including second-degree murder, and is presumed innocent of them. In September 2025, Justice Gregory Carro dismissed the state's first-degree murder charge and a second terrorism-based murder charge ([Mediaite, Sept. 16, 2025](https://www.mediaite.com/media/news/just-in-judge-dismisses-two-of-the-major-charges-against-luigi-mangione)). In June 2026, his lawyers withdrew a planned Extreme Emotional Disturbance (EED) defense in the state case. EED is an affirmative defense that, if proved, can reduce murder to manslaughter ([CNN via ABC17, June 18, 2026](https://abc17news.com/news/national-world/cnn-national/2026/06/18/luigi-mangiones-attorneys-say-they-are-withdrawing-psychiatric-defense/)).

After the federal plea, the defense filed a motion to dismiss under New York's double-jeopardy law. The state trial will no longer start on September 8, 2026 ([Courthouse News, Aug. 17, 2026](https://www.courthousenews.com/no-september-murder-trial-for-luigi-mangione-after-federal-guilty-plea/)). A court order set the prosecution's answer for October 9, a defense reply by October 30 and the next court date for December 10, 2026 ([N.Y. Supreme Court order, Aug. 17, 2026](https://www.nycourts.gov/LegacyPDFS/press/pdfs/order-081726.pdf)). Prosecutors filed their answer on October 9. They argue the state and federal crimes are "different crimes" ([CNN via ABC17, Oct. 9, 2026](https://abc17news.com/news/national-world/cnn-national/2026/10/09/state-prosecutors-argue-luigi-mangiones-different-crimes-mean-double-jeopardy-doesnt-apply-to-him/)). The judge has not ruled. No new state trial date has been set.

## The "Federal Trap"

Before the federal plea, the two cases created a strategic bind. To succeed with an EED defense in the state case, Mangione would have had to admit the killing. That admission could then have been used against him in the federal case, where New York's EED defense does not apply.

Prosecutors argued the contradiction was insurmountable: a man who manufactured a ghost gun, maintained a fake identity for ten days, used a suppressor, and executed a multi-state escape plan is not a man who lost self-control. In their view, the same careful planning that makes him sympathetic to some is the very evidence that defeats that defense. The defense withdrew the EED plan in June 2026.

## The Folk Hero Phenomenon

Perhaps more jarring than the killing was the "Free Luigi" movement that followed. A GiveSendGo fundraiser for his legal defense surpassed $1.3 million. Polls revealed a stark generational divide: a plurality of respondents aged 18–29 found the killing "acceptable or somewhat acceptable." The cultural response — viral protest songs, look-alike contests in Washington Square Park — represented propaganda of the deed in the social media age.

## The Corporate Ripple Effect

The months after the shooting were hard for the company Thompson worked for. UnitedHealth's stock reversed its earlier gains. In May 2025, UnitedHealth Group CEO Andrew Witty stepped down. The company said he left for personal reasons. It also suspended its 2025 outlook because medical costs ran higher than forecast. ## Questions people ask

### Did Luigi Mangione plead guilty to murder?

No. He pleaded guilty to two federal stalking counts that involve Brian Thompson's death. The federal murder count was dismissed in January 2026. The remaining murder charge is in the New York state case. There he has pleaded not guilty and is presumed innocent.

### Can Luigi Mangione get the death penalty?

No. On January 30, 2026, Judge Garnett dismissed Counts Three and Four of the federal indictment, a murder charge and a weapons charge. Count Three was the charge that could have carried a death sentence ([court docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69908890/united-states-v-mangione/); [WRDW, Jan. 30, 2026](https://www.wrdw.com/2026/01/30/death-penalty-dropped-luigi-mangione-case/)).

### What is double jeopardy, and why does it matter here?

Double jeopardy is the rule against being prosecuted twice for the same crime. His lawyers say New York's version of that rule bars the state case after his federal plea. Prosecutors say the state and federal cases involve different crimes. The judge has not ruled.
