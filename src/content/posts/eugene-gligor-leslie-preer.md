In May 2025, in Montgomery County Circuit Court, Eugene Gligor pleaded guilty to second-degree murder in the killing of Leslie Preer. Leslie was found dead in her Chevy Chase, Maryland, home on May 2, 2001. She was 49 years old. Some reports give her age as 50. Her case went 23 years without an arrest.

The question most people ask first is not how he was caught. The question is how he stayed free for so long.

The answer is simple and hard to sit with. Gligor was not a stranger. He was someone her family already knew. Years before the killing, he had dated Leslie's daughter. Police say he was 44 when he was arrested in June 2024. Othram's case page says he was about 20 when Leslie was killed.

His name reached the case file early. Charging documents cited by Bethesda Magazine describe a tip dated Jan. 30, 2002, about nine months after the killing. In it, a former neighbor suggested Gligor may have been involved and mentioned his past relationship with Leslie's daughter. The tip was not a charge, and it was not proof.

Think about what that means. The name Eugene Gligor did not first appear through forensic science twenty years later. It was already in the file. But the DNA from the scene did not match any of the men investigators had interviewed or tested, or anyone in the FBI's database, according to court documents cited by Bethesda Magazine. So the case went cold. The name sat in that file, waiting for science to catch up.

In this case, the lead was already in the paperwork. What was missing was proof.

Gligor went on to live his life. Fox News reports that he lived in Washington, D.C., for more than 20 years. Leslie's family lived with her loss and no answer. The case stayed with Montgomery County detectives who had not forgotten it.

In September 2022, Montgomery County police teamed with Othram, a forensic DNA lab in The Woodlands, Texas. Othram built a DNA profile from the crime scene evidence and gave it to investigators for a genetic genealogy search. Charging documents say the profile went to FamilyTreeDNA's database. Its list of DNA matches included the surname Gligor. ABC News reports that the closest match led to a family line in Romania. State's Attorney John McCarthy said the DNA led to a distant relative from Romania. Detectives then found the Gligor name in the original case file.

Next, detectives needed confirmation. In June 2024, with help from customs officials at Dulles International Airport, they collected a water bottle Gligor drank from and threw away, according to ABC News. Bethesda Magazine reports this happened during a secondary screening. Fox News, citing court filings obtained by The Washington Post, describes the screening as staged. Police say the DNA from it matched the DNA from the crime scene. The genealogy was the lead. The direct DNA match was the proof.

Eugene Gligor was arrested in June 2024 and charged with first-degree murder. On May 7, 2025, he pleaded guilty to second-degree murder in Montgomery County Circuit Court. On Aug. 28, 2025, he was sentenced to 30 years with all but 22 suspended, followed by five years of supervised probation.

The case drew national television coverage. ABC's 20/20 aired "First Comes Love, Then Comes Murder" on Sept. 12, 2025. Dateline NBC aired "A Perfect Spring Morning" on Oct. 17, 2025. Part of what makes the case hard to set aside is plain: a young man her family knew went on to kill the mother of the woman he had once dated.

The record does not explain why. The guilty plea tells us what he admitted to in court. The sentence tells us the court found 22 years to serve warranted. No record can tell us why he did it.

McCarthy called it the first time familial DNA was used to solve a cold case murder in Montgomery County. That credit belongs to the detectives who kept working, the scientists who built the DNA profile, and the genealogists who built a family tree from opt-in data. It also belongs to a family that waited 23 years for an answer.

The Leslie Preer murder case is closed. Her family has an answer, though not one that returns what they lost.
