## What Nevada’s principal law explains

[NRS 195.020, Nevada’s principal-liability statute](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/division/legal/lawlibrary/nrs/NRS-195.html), includes both the person who directly commits a felony and people who aid or abet its commission, whether present or absent. It also reaches conduct such as counseling, encouraging, hiring, commanding, inducing or procuring another person to commit the crime.

The important distinction is between **physical action** and **criminal responsibility**. Under this rule, liability can extend beyond the person who performed the direct act. A relationship, group association or physical proximity is not a substitute for proving the defendant’s criminal responsibility.

That is a general explanation of the statute, not a reconstruction of Davis’s trial. The state must prove the charged offense beyond a reasonable doubt under the applicable law and jury instructions. Without examining those instructions and the verdict form, we should not assign a particular theory to the jurors.

The same caution applies to the shooter question. The reported conviction alone is not a basis for NEP to declare who personally fired, or to declare that the jury made no finding about that. Reading the actual record is different from filling its gaps with an old interview, allegation or theory.

## Four decisions to keep separate

- **A charge** states an accusation. It is not a verdict.
- **A verdict** records the jury’s decision on the charges submitted to it. The accompanying instructions and verdict form help explain what was decided.
- **A sentence** sets the punishment. Reporting about the sentencing hearing and the filed written judgment serve different record-checking purposes.
- **An appeal** seeks review. Announcing one is different from filing a notice, and a notice is different from an appellate decision.

A reported conviction is a substantial change in legal status. It does not make every surrounding public claim a court finding. Equally, an intention to appeal does not erase the reported conviction or sentence.

## Why “40 years to life” is not a release date

A years-to-life term has a stated minimum and a maximum of life. It does not promise release at the end of the stated number of years. NEP is not supplying a parole year or a calculation based on an assumed start date.

The written judgment is the place to check the sentence terms the court entered. Any parole-eligibility calculation may require additional official records and applicable law. We have not verified either the exact sentence-component breakdown or an official eligibility calculation.

Current penalty provisions also require care. [Nevada’s deadly-weapon enhancement statute, NRS 193.165](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/Division/Legal/LawLibrary/NRS/NRS-193.html), has changed since the 1996 killing. Today’s statutory wording should not automatically be substituted for the law applicable to that offense. Current penalty options are not proof of the punishment actually imposed.

## Match the question to the record

Use this short reading map before treating a new headline as a complete answer:

- **What legal theory was submitted to the jury?** Read the jury instructions and verdict form. Do not assume they will resolve every factual question about the shooting.
- **What sentence did the court enter?** Read the filed judgment of conviction. Distinguish its terms from a news summary.
- **Was an appeal filed?** Look for the notice filed in the district court; later appellate case history helps track review. A lawyer’s announced intention is a different kind of information.
- **What did an appeal decide?** Read the appellate decision and the issues it addresses; a filing is not a ruling.

The [Nevada Rules of Appellate Procedure, Rules 3 and 4](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/CourtRules/NRAP.html), govern taking an appeal and its timing. Rule 4 ties ordinary criminal-appeal timing to entry of judgment, with rules for certain post-trial motions. Do not calculate a case deadline from the hearing date alone.

This map is also useful in other cases: identify the question, find the record capable of addressing it, and keep any remaining uncertainty visible.
