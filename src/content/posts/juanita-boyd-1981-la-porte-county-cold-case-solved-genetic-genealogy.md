Juanita Boyd lived in La Porte, Indiana. On February 22, 1981, she was reported missing after she did not come home.

Six days later, on February 28, her car, a green Pontiac she had recently bought, was found stuck in mud on a farm lane in rural Cool Spring Township. Juanita was in the trunk. Her death was ruled a homicide by asphyxiation. For 45 years, no one was named.

## What investigators say changed

In November 2026, the La Porte County Sheriff's Office announced that the case has a suspect. Investigators say DNA recovered from Juanita's clothing matched a man named Carl David Phillips, and that genetic genealogy helped lead them to him ([FOX 32 Chicago](https://www.fox32chicago.com/news/indiana-cold-case-solved-suspect-identified-in-womans-1981-murder), with matching reports from WBBM Newsradio).

Phillips died on September 24, 2017. Investigators say he would have been charged with Juanita's murder if he were alive. Because he is dead, there will be no charges and no trial.

## What is still not known

Investigators say there is no known connection between Phillips and Juanita. They believe the two had some kind of interaction, and they point to circumstantial evidence that placed him in the area around the time of the murder. Phillips was never charged in this case. Reports differ on his age, so we are not printing one.

## Why this matters

Genetic genealogy builds a family tree from DNA. It does not name a person by itself. Investigators still have to match the result to evidence, and here they say they did. For Juanita's family, a case that went quiet in 1981 now has an answer.

Juanita Boyd was a person first, and she was remembered by the people who loved her long after the case went cold.
