Jake Robinson, Callum Robinson, and Jack Carter Rhoad were camping and surfing near Punta San José on Baja California’s Pacific coast when they disappeared after their last contact around April 27, 2024. Their bodies were later recovered from a well or shaft several miles from the campsite.

The judge-only trial began on September 7, 2026, in Ensenada. That distinction matters: a judge, rather than a jury, will decide the case. The three defendants are presumed innocent. A charge is a formal accusation, not a finding of guilt.

The first two days have been about testimony entering the record. A protected witness testified about travel to the Punta San José area, what he later saw, sounds he heard, and a warning not to speak. A friend and colleague testified about the search that followed unanswered messages, including using Carter’s phone location as a guide. Those accounts are evidence for the judge to weigh, not a verdict.

Prosecutors say the deaths arose from an attempt to steal a pickup truck, tires, and belongings. That is the prosecution’s allegation, not an established finding. Public reporting has not established who fired the fatal shots, whether every allegation will be proven, or when a verdict will be reached.

This opening-days account uses reporting available through September 10, 2026; the courtroom evidence covered here runs through ABC’s reporting on trial day two, September 9, 2026.
