*Status as of Oct. 31, 2026 (Pacific).* On Oct. 9, 2026, the trial judge in Ensenada found three men guilty of the murders of Jake Robinson, Callum Robinson and Jack Carter Rhoad, and of armed robbery and armed carjacking ([Al Jazeera, Oct. 10, 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/10/mexican-judge-sentences-three-men-for-murders-of-australian-us-surfers)). On Oct. 16, the judge sentenced each man to 158 years and nine months in prison and ordered compensation to the families ([AAP via Illawarra Mercury, Oct. 17, 2026](https://www.illawarramercury.com.au/story/9370247/trio-sentenced-to-158-years-for-killing-aussie-surfers/)). Their lawyers say they will appeal. This is the trial court's judgment, not a final one. A higher court can still review it on appeal. The account below covers only the first two days of the trial, through Sept. 10, 2026.

Jake Robinson, Callum Robinson, and Jack Carter Rhoad were camping and surfing near Punta San José on Baja California’s Pacific coast when they disappeared after their last contact around April 27, 2024. Their bodies were later recovered from a well or shaft several miles from the campsite.

The judge-only trial began on September 7, 2026, in Ensenada. That distinction matters: a judge, rather than a jury, decided the case. During the trial, the three defendants were presumed innocent.

The first two days have been about testimony entering the record. A protected witness testified about travel to the Punta San José area, what he later saw, sounds he heard, and a warning not to speak. A friend and colleague testified about the search that followed unanswered messages, including using Carter’s phone location as a guide. Those accounts are evidence for the judge to weigh, not a verdict.

Prosecutors say the deaths arose from an attempt to steal a pickup truck, tires, and belongings. That is the prosecution’s allegation, not an established finding. At that point, public reporting had not established who fired the fatal shots or whether every allegation would be proven.

This opening-days account uses reporting available through September 10, 2026; the courtroom evidence covered here runs through ABC’s reporting on trial day two, September 9, 2026.
